## From hunspell

  
Bring your own dictionaries. Register the .aff/.dic pair you already have and
nothing is re-downloaded:

  
```text
kotoshu setup en --aff en_US.aff --dic en_US.dic

# or point at a directory — it expects LANG.aff and LANG.dic
kotoshu setup en --from /usr/share/hunspell/
```

  
Because the engine is a Ruby port of the Hunspell algorithm — with behavior
tested against Hunspell's own reference fixtures — affix rules, flag
conditions, and compound handling behave the way your dictionaries expect.
The difference you will notice is operational, not linguistic: resources are
explicit (setup once, cache-only after) and results come out as JSON or SARIF
in addition to text.

  
## From aspell

  
Kotoshu does not read aspell's compiled .rws dictionaries; point it at
Hunspell-format files or a plain word list instead
(`dictionary_type :plain_text`
or `:hunspell`). Aspell
personal word lists (.pws) are not auto-imported yet — export yours as one
word per line and import it once:

  
```text
kotoshu personal import my-words.txt
```

  
After that, `kotoshu personal add`
and the CLI's interactive review keep the personal dictionary current by hand.

  
## From cspell

  
cspell's configuration maps cleanly onto Kotoshu's model, with one honest gap:
cspell's committed-in-repo cspell.json ergonomics has no Kotoshu equivalent
yet — configuration lives in XDG files and environment variables.

## From LanguageTool

  
Be clear-eyed here: Kotoshu does not compete on grammar. LanguageTool's rule
library — thousands of grammar, punctuation, and style rules across dozens of
languages — has no equivalent in Kotoshu today; grammar rule packs are a
roadmap item, not a feature. What Kotoshu offers is spelling with optional
semantic reranking of suggestions, resource downloads on demand, and a CI
story built on exit codes and SARIF. If you need grammar now, run both side
by side and let each do its half.
